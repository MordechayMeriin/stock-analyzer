Israeli market context (applies to every company you research):

Sources
- Business press: Globes (globes.co.il, en.globes.co.il), Calcalist (calcalist.co.il, calcalistech.com),
  TheMarker (themarker.com), Bizportal (bizportal.co.il), Ice (ice.co.il), Maariv/Ynet/Walla business
  sections, The Times of Israel and Jerusalem Post business, Reuters and Bloomberg for larger companies.
- Market data: the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange (tase.co.il, market.tase.co.il) for prices, indices (TA-35,
  TA-125, sector indices), corporate bonds and the Tel-Bond indices; Bizportal and Globes quote pages;
  short-interest and fund-holding trackers. Most Israeli corporate bonds are shekel-denominated and many
  are CPI-linked, so compare yields with Israeli government bonds of similar duration.
- Ratings: S&P Maalot and Midroog rate most Israeli issuers on the local scale (ilAAA, Aa1.il, ...);
  larger companies may also have S&P, Moody's or Fitch ratings. Rating actions and outlook changes are
  reported by the business press and on the agencies' sites.
- Analysts: Israeli brokerages and investment houses (Leader Capital Markets, IBI, Psagot, Meitav,
  Altshuler Shaham, Bank Leumi and Poalim research) and foreign banks for dual-listed companies.
- Government and regulators: Bank of Israel (rates, banking supervision), Israel Securities Authority
  (enforcement news, not filings), Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority, Competition Authority,
  Central Bureau of Statistics, the relevant ministry and sector regulator, Knesset committees, courts.
- Do not use the company's official annual or quarterly reports, or the filing systems Maya and Magna.

What tends to matter for Israeli companies
- Ownership: many companies have a controlling shareholder, sometimes through a pyramid of listed holding
  companies. The controller's debt, disputes, sales of shares or pledges affect the company.
- Concentration: a small domestic market, so a few competitors per sector, active antitrust enforcement
  and strong regulators. Concessions, licences and government tenders are common.
- Labor: the Histadrut and workers' committees are strong at large and formerly state-owned companies.
- Macro: Bank of Israel interest rate, inflation and CPI-linked debt, the shekel against the dollar and
  euro (exporters versus importers), housing and construction cycles, credit conditions.
- Security and geopolitics: wars, rocket fire, reserve-duty call-ups, airspace or port closures, travel
  warnings, sanctions, boycotts and divestment campaigns, and Israel's sovereign rating all hit companies
  unevenly by sector.

Sector guide: pick the indicators for the company's industry
- Banks: credit growth, credit-loss provisions and problem loans, capital adequacy and regulatory
  pressure from the Supervisor of Banks, fees and competition reforms, mortgages.
- Insurers and pension funds: capital ratios (Solvency), market share in long-term savings, claims and
  catastrophe exposure, investment returns, Capital Market Authority actions.
- Real estate and construction: sales and price data, land tenders, project pipeline and permits,
  financing costs, contractor and labor shortages, the housing market.
- Technology: customers and contracts, layoffs or hiring, funding and valuations, export markets,
  dual listing on Nasdaq or NYSE, security-related disruption to staff.
- Telecom and media: subscribers and churn, price competition, the Ministry of Communications, fiber
  and spectrum, structural reforms.
- Retail and consumer: same-store sales, prices and the cost-of-living debate, import competition,
  supplier disputes.
- Energy and infrastructure: gas fields and exports, power-plant projects, tariffs set by the
  Electricity Authority, concessions, renewable tenders.
- Defense and industry: order backlog and new contracts, export permits, government budgets.
- Transport and tourism (airlines, shipping, ports): traffic statistics (for example the Israel Airports
  Authority or ports data), capacity and fleet, fuel costs, foreign competitors, security disruptions.
- Healthcare and pharma: approvals, pricing, clinical results, the national health basket.
For any other industry, identify the two or three numbers its managers and analysts watch, and track them.
